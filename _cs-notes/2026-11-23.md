# How your phone finds a website (DNS)

Computers talk in numbers (IP addresses), but people remember names like example.com.
DNS, the Domain Name System, is the internet's contacts list: you give it a name, it gives back a number.

- Your device asks a resolver (usually your ISP's or 1.1.1.1 / 8.8.8.8).
- The resolver asks root, then .com, then the site's own name server, until it gets the IP.
- Answers are cached for a set time (TTL), so repeat visits are fast.
- When DNS goes down, sites seem "dead" even though their servers are fine.
